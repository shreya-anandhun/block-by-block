02 · The secret sauce

Hashes, keys & signatures

Three pieces of maths quietly power every Web3 app. A hash fingerprints data, a key pair proves who you are, and a signature lets you approve things without ever revealing your secret. Play with each one below.

01SHA-256 02Keys 03Signatures 04Why it matters
Explain it
01

The fingerprint machine

SHA-256

SHA-256 is a cryptographic hash function: a one-way blender for data. Pour in anything, a single word or an entire book, and it always gives back a fingerprint of exactly the same size: 256 bits, written as 64 characters.

SHA-256 (Secure Hash Algorithm, 256-bit) splits input into 512-bit chunks and runs each through 64 rounds of bit rotations, additions and logic. The output is always a 256-bit digest (32 bytes, 64 hex characters). It is deterministic, one-way (you can’t reverse it) and collision-resistant (no one has found two inputs with the same hash).

  • Same input, same hash. Every time, on every computer.
  • Any size in, 256 bits out. A letter or a library, same length.
  • Tiny change, totally new hash. This is called the avalanche effect.
  • No going back. You can’t work out the input from the hash.

In blockchains, every block stores the hash of the block before it. Change one old transaction and its hash changes, breaking every link after it.

02

Your identity, in three steps

Cryptographic keys

Your Web3 identity starts with a huge random number: your private key. From it, maths creates a public key, and from that, a short wallet address you can share. Each arrow only works one way, so nobody can travel backwards from your address to your secret.

The private key is a random 256-bit number. The public key is that number multiplied by a fixed point on the secp256k1 elliptic curve, which is easy forwards and practically impossible to reverse. Ethereum then hashes the public key with Keccak-256 and keeps the last 20 bytes as your address; Bitcoin uses SHA-256 plus RIPEMD-160.

03

Approve without revealing

Digital signatures

When you send a transaction, your wallet uses your private key to create a signature: a unique stamp made from both your key and the exact message. Anyone can check the stamp with your public key and know it was you, but the stamp never reveals the key itself.

Wallets sign with ECDSA (or Schnorr in newer Bitcoin): the transaction is hashed, then combined with the private key and a one-time random number to produce a signature pair (r, s). Verifiers use the public key to check that pair against the hash. Change one byte of the transaction and the hash changes, so the old signature no longer fits.

  1. 1
    Write the transaction.
  2. 2
    Sign it inside your wallet with the private key.
  3. 3
    Broadcast the message + signature. The key stays home.
  4. 4
    Verify: every node checks it with your public key.

Why it matters in Web3

Ownership

Whoever holds the private key controls the address and everything in it. No bank account, no company login: the key is the ownership.

private key → address → your assets

Authentication

Signatures replace passwords. Apps check a signature against your public key to know it’s really you, and your secret never leaves your wallet.

sign → verify → access granted

Integrity

Hashes lock every transaction and block in place. Change a single character anywhere and the fingerprints stop matching, so everyone can spot it.

data → hash → chain of blocks

The golden rule: lose your private key and nobody can recover it; share it and anyone can take everything. That is why wallets hide it behind a seed phrase, and why no real app will ever ask you for it.

01 / 06 · How it works

Decentralisation

Decentralisation eliminates the need for a powerful middleman like a bank or a massive tech corporation by spreading control across a global network of independent computers. Instead of storing data on a single corporate server, information is copied and secured across thousands of devices simultaneously using blockchain technology. Every member of this network follows the exact same set of automated rules to verify and agree on updates. This structure ensures that no single entity can shut down the platform, censor information, or alter history without the network's consensus. Ultimately, it gives individual users total ownership over their own digital identity, data, and assets.

Key termsNodeA computer that keeps a full copy of the ledger and checks every rule.Distributed ledgerOne shared record, copied across thousands of nodes at once.Peer-to-peerComputers talk to each other directly, with no central server in between.Censorship resistanceNo single party can block, delete or rewrite a valid transaction.

02 / 06 · How it works

Consensus

When new data is added, individual computers across the network independently audit it using math-based rules like Proof of Work or Proof of Stake to prevent fraud. Once a majority of these computers agree the information is accurate, the transaction is approved and permanently locked into a shared digital ledger. This automated collective agreement ensures absolute trust and security across the entire platform without relying on a single boss.

Key termsValidatorA node that checks new blocks and votes on whether they are valid.Proof of WorkMiners spend computing power (electricity) to earn the right to add a block.Proof of StakeValidators lock up coins as a deposit; cheating gets that stake “slashed”.FinalityThe point after which a confirmed block can no longer be reversed.

03 / 06 · How it works

Smart contracts

These digital agreements run securely on a blockchain network, completely eliminating the need for traditional middlemen like lawyers or escrow agents. They operate on simple “if/then” logic, meaning that as soon as the required conditions are verified, the contract instantly triggers the next action such as releasing funds or transferring digital property. Because the code is stored permanently on a shared ledger, it cannot be tampered with, altered, or stopped by any single party. This automation ensures that transactions are fast, transparent, and completely accurate without the risk of human error or bias.

Key termsIf/then logicThe contract’s terms: when a condition is met, an action runs automatically.GasThe fee paid to the network to run a contract’s code.ImmutableOnce deployed, the code cannot be edited or stopped by one party.SolidityThe most common language smart contracts are written in.

04 / 06 · How it works

Tokens & NFTs

Built on blockchain networks, standard tokens are fungible assets that hold identical value and can be swapped one-for-one to handle payments or voting within an ecosystem. In contrast, Non-Fungible Tokens (NFTs) are completely unique digital deeds that cannot be exchanged equally because each holds distinct characteristics and data. Together, they form the economy of the decentralised web by letting users trade everyday currency-like tokens while securely owning individual assets like digital art, collectibles, or virtual real estate. This dual system allows platforms to fully automate both general financial transactions and exclusive property rights without relying on central authorities.

Key termsFungibleInterchangeable: any one unit is worth exactly the same as another.ERC-20Ethereum’s standard for fungible tokens.ERC-721Ethereum’s standard for NFTs, each with its own unique ID.MintingCreating a brand-new token or NFT on the blockchain.

05 / 06 · How it works

DAOs

Instead of relying on a traditional CEO, these groups use smart contracts to automate operations and manage resources based on pre-set code. Decisions are made democratically by members who use governance tokens to propose changes and vote on the organisation's future. Every transaction and vote is permanently recorded on a public blockchain, ensuring total transparency for everyone involved. This allows global communities of strangers to securely pool funds in a shared treasury and collaborate on projects without needing a central coordinator or legal middleman.

Key termsGovernance tokenA token that gives its holder votes on the DAO’s decisions.ProposalA suggested change that members vote on.TreasuryThe DAO’s shared funds, controlled by smart contracts.QuorumThe minimum number of votes needed for a decision to count.

06 / 06 · How it works

Wallets & keys

A crypto wallet does not actually store coins internally; instead, it holds the cryptographic keys needed to access and manage your assets on the public blockchain. Your public key acts like a digital email address that anyone can see to send you funds, while your private key serves as the secret password required to sign off on outgoing transactions. If you lose your private key, you permanently lose access to your funds, and if anyone else sees it, they can instantly steal your entire balance. This system gives you absolute, independent ownership over your wealth, completely eliminating the need for a traditional bank to safeguard your account.

Key termsPublic keyShareable. Your address is derived from it so others can send you funds.Private keySecret. It signs your transactions; whoever has it controls the funds.Seed phrase12 or 24 words that back up and restore all of your keys.Digital signatureProof, made with your private key, that you approved a transaction.

What just happened

  • No password was created. The page asked your wallet for permission; the wallet returned the address it is willing to reveal. That is the entire handshake.
  • Your key never moved. Private keys never leave the wallet. Websites can only ever ask it to sign something, and you approve or reject each request.

07 · Decoder

Crack the code words

Ten terms you will see in every thread, every whitepaper and every scam. Pick one to decode it in plain English, with a picture.

Basics 1 / 10

Address

In plain words

Your public account number on the blockchain. Share it freely so people can send you tokens or NFTs.

Think of it like

Your email address, but for money.

Example

0x7a3F9c2E…b41D9c2E

Money 2 / 10

Gas & gwei

In plain words

The small fee you pay the network to process your transaction. It is priced in tiny slices of ETH called gwei.

Think of it like

Postage on a letter: busier days cost a little more.

Example

Fee: 0.00042 ETH (about 12 gwei)

Tech 3 / 10

EVM

In plain words

The Ethereum Virtual Machine: one shared computer that every node runs, so the same code always gives the same result.

Think of it like

One calculator copied onto thousands of desks: same buttons, same answer.

Example

2 + 40 → 42 on every node

Safety · handle with care 4 / 10

Seed phrase

In plain words

12 or 24 ordinary words that can restore your entire wallet. Whoever has these words has your money.

Think of it like

The master key to every door you own. Keep it on paper, never online.

Example

apple · river · candle · … (never share!)

Tech 5 / 10

Node

In plain words

A computer that keeps a full copy of the blockchain and checks every rule. Most apps talk to one through a service called an RPC provider.

Think of it like

One of thousands of librarians, each holding the exact same book.

Example

~8,000+ nodes worldwide

Tech 6 / 10

Mempool

In plain words

The waiting room where new transactions sit until they get picked into a block. It is public, so anyone can peek.

Think of it like

A queue at the ticket counter. Paying a higher fee lets you skip ahead.

Example

3 pending · next block in ~12s

Basics 7 / 10

Finality

In plain words

The moment a transaction can no longer be reversed. Before it, it is pending. After it, it is done for good.

Think of it like

Wet cement that has fully set.

Example

Ethereum: final after about 13 minutes

Basics 8 / 10

Testnet

In plain words

A practice copy of the blockchain where the coins are free and worthless, so you can learn and make mistakes safely.

Think of it like

A flight simulator: real controls, no real crash.

Example

Sepolia · coins from a free “faucet”

Safety · handle with care 9 / 10

Bridge

In plain words

A service that moves tokens from one blockchain to another. Useful, but bridges hold huge amounts of money, so hackers love them.

Think of it like

A currency exchange at the airport: handy, and a big target.

Example

ETH on Ethereum → ETH on Base

Safety · handle with care 10 / 10

Rug pull

In plain words

A scam where a project’s team collects everyone’s money and then disappears with it.

Think of it like

A shop that takes deposits, then closes overnight and vanishes.

Example

Red flags: anonymous team · “guaranteed” returns · rush to buy
